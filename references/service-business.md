# Service Business Operations

## Service Models

### Hourly / Time-Based
- Bill by the hour or day
- Simple, clients understand it
- Downside: income tied to time, hard to scale
- Best for: consulting, freelancing, getting started
- Track time meticulously (Toggl, Clockify -- both free)

### Project-Based / Fixed Price
- Scope defined upfront, fixed price agreed
- Better margins if you scope well, risky if you underestimate
- Requires detailed scope document before starting
- Best for: web development, design, marketing campaigns
- Always include revision limits and out-of-scope clauses

### Retainer / Subscription
- Fixed monthly fee for ongoing access or deliverables
- Predictable revenue, lower churn than project work
- Example: "$2,000/month for 20 hours of marketing support"
- Best for: established client relationships, ongoing needs
- Transition project clients to retainers after first successful project

### Productized Service
- Standardized offering at fixed price (not custom scoping)
- Example: "Logo design -- $499, delivered in 5 days, 2 revision rounds"
- Scalable through SOPs, templates, and eventually delegation
- Best for: repeatable services with predictable scope
- Price based on value delivered, not time spent

## Scoping and Proposals

### Discovery Process
1. Understand the client's problem (not their requested solution)
2. Ask: What does success look like? What is this worth to you?
3. Identify scope, timeline, budget, decision-maker
4. Qualify: Can you actually deliver this well? Is the budget realistic?

### Proposal Structure
```
1. Executive Summary (their problem, your understanding)
2. Proposed Solution (what you will do, not how)
3. Deliverables (specific, measurable outputs)
4. Timeline (milestones with dates)
5. Investment (pricing -- never call it "cost")
6. Terms (payment schedule, revision policy, cancellation)
7. Next Steps (how to approve and get started)
```

### Pricing the Proposal
- Never price before understanding scope
- Provide 2-3 options (good/better/best) -- most pick the middle
- Anchor with the premium option first
- Include a "quick win" option for price-sensitive clients
- Payment terms: 50% upfront, 50% on delivery (or 30/30/40 for larger projects)

### Scope Creep Prevention
- Document everything in writing before starting
- Define what is included AND what is not included
- Revision limits: "Includes 2 rounds of revisions. Additional revisions at $X/hour."
- Change request process: any scope change requires written approval and pricing adjustment
- Do not start additional work without signed change order

## Client Management

### Communication
- Set expectations on response time upfront (e.g., "within 24 hours on business days")
- Weekly status updates for active projects (even if just "on track, no blockers")
- Use one channel consistently (email or project tool -- not scattered across text, Slack, email)
- Document all decisions in writing. Verbal agreements become disputes.

### Onboarding
- Welcome email with: what to expect, timeline, what you need from them, key contacts
- Collect everything you need upfront (access, assets, brand guidelines, preferences)
- Set a kickoff meeting or call to align on goals and process
- Provide a shared workspace (Google Drive folder, Notion page, or project tool)

### Handling Difficult Clients
- Late payers: enforce payment terms. Stop work if payment is 15+ days overdue.
- Scope creepers: refer to the signed scope. Offer a change order with pricing.
- Unresponsive clients: "I need X by [date] to stay on timeline. If I do not hear back, I will proceed with [default decision]."
- Unhappy clients: listen first, empathize, offer a specific fix. If the relationship is unrecoverable, offer a professional exit.

### Firing a Client
Sometimes necessary. Do it professionally:
- "I do not think I am the best fit for this project going forward."
- Offer a transition period or referral to another provider
- Never burn bridges. Industries are small.

## Delivery and Quality

### Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs)
- Document the process for every repeatable service
- Include: tools used, steps in order, quality checkpoints, delivery format
- SOPs enable delegation and consistency
- Update after every project where something went wrong

### Quality Control
- Internal review before client delivery
- Checklist for every deliverable type
- Ask: "Would I be proud to show this to a prospective client?"
- Deliver early when possible. Under-promise, over-deliver.

### Handoff
- Deliver in the agreed format (not a format convenient for you)
- Include documentation or instructions if the client needs to maintain it
- Transfer ownership of all assets (files, access, accounts)
- Send a summary: what was delivered, what was decided, what happens next

## Scaling a Service Business

### Stage 1: Do Everything Yourself
- Learn every aspect of delivery
- Build SOPs as you go
- Target: $5k-10k/month solo

### Stage 2: Delegate Delivery
- Hire contractors for repeatable tasks
- You handle sales, scoping, quality control, client relationships
- Margin: keep 40-60% after contractor costs
- Target: $10k-25k/month with 2-5 contractors

### Stage 3: Productize
- Turn your most popular service into a fixed-price, fixed-scope offering
- Market it like a product (landing page, pricing, self-serve checkout)
- Reduce custom work, increase standardized delivery
- Target: $25k-50k/month

### Stage 4: Systemize
- Hire an account manager to handle clients
- You focus on marketing, strategy, and growth
- The business runs without your daily involvement
- Target: $50k+/month

## Metrics

| Metric | Formula | Target |
|--------|---------|--------|
| Utilization rate | Billable hours / Available hours | 60-75% |
| Average project value | Revenue / Projects completed | Increase over time |
| Client lifetime value | Total revenue from client / Time as client | Grow with retainers |
| Proposal win rate | Won / Proposals sent | 30-50% |
| Client satisfaction | NPS or 1-5 rating survey | > 8/10 or > 50 NPS |
| Revenue per employee | Total revenue / Team size | $100k+ per person |
