# Pricing Strategies

## Frameworks

### Value-Based Pricing
Price based on the value delivered to the customer, not your cost to produce.
- "This tool saves you 10 hours/month" = price at a fraction of what those hours are worth
- Trades contractor billing $75/hour, saving 10 hours = $750 value. Price at $29-79/month.
- Always anchor to the cost of NOT using your product

### Competitive Pricing
- Identify what the market leader charges at each tier
- Price 30-50% below for comparable features (challenger positioning)
- Or price premium with clear differentiation (quality positioning)
- Never be the middle -- be clearly cheaper or clearly better

### Freemium
- Free tier should be genuinely useful, not crippled
- Free users generate: data, word-of-mouth, content, community, future upgrades
- Free-to-paid conversion target: 2-5% is good, 5-10% is great
- Upgrade trigger should be natural (team size, usage limit, premium feature)

### Tiered Pricing
Structure tiers around distinct customer segments:
- **Starter/Free:** Individual, basic features, limited usage
- **Professional:** Power user or small team, full features
- **Business/Team:** Multiple users, collaboration, integrations, priority support
- Each tier should feel like an obvious choice for its target segment

## Pricing Psychology

- **Anchor high:** Show the expensive plan first. The mid-tier looks reasonable by comparison.
- **Odd pricing:** $29 beats $30. $79 beats $80. The left digit matters most.
- **Annual discount:** Offer 15-20% off for annual billing. Improves cash flow and reduces churn.
- **Per-seat vs flat:** Per-seat works for collaboration tools. Flat works for individual tools.
- **Usage-based:** Good for infrastructure. Aligns cost with value. Harder to predict revenue.

## Pricing Mistakes

- Pricing too low. Underpricing signals low quality and leaves money on the table.
- Pricing too high for the market stage. Early stage = maximize adoption, not revenue per user.
- Changing prices on existing customers without notice. Grandfather existing customers or give 90 days notice.
- Too many tiers. Three is the sweet spot. More than four creates decision paralysis.
- Hiding pricing. If you do not show pricing on your website, you lose 80% of potential customers who will not bother to "contact sales."

## Testing Pricing

- Test on new cohorts, never on existing customers
- A/B test landing pages with different price points
- Watch for: conversion rate changes, trial-to-paid changes, support ticket volume, cancellation reasons
- If raising price does not reduce conversions proportionally, raise it more
- The right price is the highest price where conversion rate stays acceptable
